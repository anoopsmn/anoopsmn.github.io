title: Sometimes the manager has to take the hit
---

Performance ratings are one of those things that can make a manager
popular for about five minutes and unpopular for much longer. At one
point, I was responsible for performance ratings across much of the
engineering organization. I ended up taking responsibility for most of
the final calls.

It wasn't always comfortable.

## Everyone has a view

People have different ideas about what good performance looks like. A
manager sees one part of someone's work. A lead sees another. Someone
working closely with that person may see something completely different.

That's one reason I introduced more structured feedback around the
organization, including input from leads and managers and broader
feedback for recognition. Over time, that wider effort was part of what
helped bring attrition down. Regular, honest conversations about where
someone actually stood mattered more than any single rating.

## More input makes the decision better, not easier

Feedback can make a decision better informed. It doesn't make the
decision disappear. At the end, someone still has to make the call,
explain the reasoning and be open to questions about it.

There were times when I absorbed most of the frustration around a final
decision. That was uncomfortable, but it was also my responsibility to
make sure the process was fair and that the reasoning could be
explained.

## Fair doesn't mean everyone agrees

One thing I learned is that transparency and agreement are different
things. You can make the inputs clearer. You can make the process more
consistent. You can explain how a decision was reached. Someone can
still walk away disappointed.

The answer isn't to avoid making the decision. It's to make sure the
decision is grounded in evidence, applied consistently and communicated
respectfully.

## Owning the decision

A manager shouldn't measure success by whether everyone likes the
outcome. The responsibility is to make the best decision possible with
the information available, explain it honestly, and look at the process
again when feedback shows it could be better.

That doesn't make difficult ratings pleasant. It does make them easier
to stand behind.
